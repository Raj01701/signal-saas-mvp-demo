# Knowledge base

Classical Jyotish rules encoded as data:

- [`yogas/`](yogas): 312 yogas and doshas (milestone M4), evaluated by
  `jyotish_engine.rules.yogas.compute_yogas`.
- [`natal/`](natal): 375 natal readings (milestone M8), the result of each placement: every
  house lord in every house (BPHS), every graha in every house (Phaladeepika) and sign
  (Brihat Jataka), the Moon's nakshatra (Brihat Jataka) and the rising sign. Evaluated by
  `jyotish_engine.rules.readings.compute_readings`.
- Dasha and transit results follow later in M8.

The engine loads every `yogas/**/*.yaml` and `natal/**/*.yaml` file, checks each rule and
citation, and evaluates the rules against a chart.
Each result carries the evidence that made the rule true, its cancellation if any,
the planets involved and its citations.

## Rules for contributors

- **Paraphrase, never copy.** Sanskrit originals are public domain; modern English
  translations are usually copyrighted. Write each rule and its effect in your own words.
- **Always cite.** Every rule lists its sources: a `text` id from
  [`sources.yaml`](sources.yaml), and for classical texts an `edition` from that entry,
  because chapter numbering differs between editions. Give a `chapter` (and `verses` when
  known) or a `locator` (a section or yoga name). Keep `verified: false` until someone
  has checked the citation against that edition.
- **Record provenance.**
  - `classical`: found in the classical texts (BPHS, Brihat Jataka, Phaladeepika, ...).
  - `traditional`: part of the received tradition and documented by modern authors such
    as B.V. Raman, but its classical source has not been identified yet.
  - `modern`: introduced by 20th-century and later practice, for example Kala Sarpa Yoga,
    which is absent from BPHS.
- **Ship tests.** Every rule has at least one chart where it must be present and one where
  it must be absent; a rule with `cancel_when` also needs a chart where it is cancelled.
- **Review status.** Rules start as `status: draft`. Only a qualified Jyotishi moves a rule
  to `reviewed`. Draft rules are hidden from consumer readings.
- **Sensitive topics.** Rules about disease, death or longevity set `sensitive: true`; they
  are left out of results unless explicitly requested (pro view only).

## Rule format

```yaml
defaults:            # merged into every rule of the file
  category: chandra
  school: parashari
  provenance: classical

rules:
  - id: chandra.gajakesari          # family.name, lower case
    name: Gajakesari Yoga
    description: >-
      Jupiter occupies a kendra from the Moon. It is cancelled when Jupiter is
      debilitated, combust or in an enemy's sign.
    when: in_kendra(jupiter, moon)
    cancel_when: debilitated(jupiter) or combust(jupiter) or enemy_sign(jupiter)
    participants: "[jupiter, moon]"  # planets whose dashas activate the rule
    effects:
      domains: [fame, wealth, intellect]
      polarity: positive             # positive | negative | mixed
      strength: major                # minor | moderate | major
      summary: Intelligence, eloquence, lasting fame and prosperity.
    sources:
      - {text: phaladeepika, edition: sastri, chapter: 6, verified: false}
    tests:
      positive:
        - {lagna: aries, moon: aries, jupiter: cancer, rest: leo}
      negative:
        - {lagna: aries, moon: aries, jupiter: taurus, rest: leo}
      cancelled:
        - {lagna: aries, moon: cancer, jupiter: capricorn, rest: leo}
```

Other fields: `aliases`, `status`, `sensitive`, `notes`. Unknown fields are rejected.
Quote an expression that starts with `[`, and use a folded block (`>-`) for text or
expressions containing `: `, which YAML would otherwise read as a mapping.

**Families** are templates plus instances. Strings in the template may contain
`{name}` placeholders, optionally with a filter: `{house|ord}` gives "7th",
`{planet|title}` gives "Mars", `{x|upper}` upper-cases. Each instance supplies `params`,
its own `tests`, and optional `overrides` of whole fields:

```yaml
families:
  - template:
      id: mahapurusha.{yoga}
      name: "{yoga|title} Yoga"
      when: in_kendra({planet}) and (own_sign({planet}) or exalted({planet}))
      ...
    instances:
      - params: {yoga: ruchaka, planet: mars}
        overrides: {effects: {...}}
        tests: {positive: [...], negative: [...]}
```

**Test charts** are compact specs. A position is a sign, optionally with degrees
(`capricorn 12`; 15 by default), or an absolute sidereal longitude. `rest` places every
graha not named. Rahu and Ketu stay opposite: give one of them, or neither (Rahu then
goes to `rest`). Optional keys: `retrograde: [saturn]`, `day_birth: true`,
`gender: female`. Specs do not enforce astronomy (Mercury may be far from the Sun), so
keep them to what the rule needs.

## Expression language

`when`, `cancel_when` and `participants` are expressions in a small language evaluated
by a safe interpreter (never Python's `eval`). Names and function arities are checked
when the catalogue loads.

- **Values:** numbers, quoted strings, lists `[...]`, planets (`sun` ... `ketu`),
  `lagna`, signs (`aries` ... `pisces`).
- **Groups:** `benefics` and `malefics` (natural, for this chart: the Moon by paksha,
  Mercury by its company; nodes are malefic), `seven` (Sun to Saturn), `grahas` (all
  nine), `nodes`, and the house groups `kendras`, `trikonas`, `dusthanas`, `upachayas`.
- **Operators:** `and`, `or`, `not`, `==`, `!=`, `<`, `<=`, `>`, `>=`, `in`, `not in`,
  and parentheses.
- **Comprehensions:** `any(p in seven: exalted(p))`, `all(...)`, `count(...)` (a number)
  and `select(...)` (a list).
- **Rule references:** `fires('nabhasa.akriti.*')` is true when another rule matching
  the pattern is present; the catalogue orders rules so that references run first.
- Houses are whole-sign houses counted from the lagna unless a reference point is
  given, for example `house(jupiter, moon)`. Aspects are Parashari graha drishti by sign.
- `strong(p)` is a working test (not debilitated or combust, and either dignified or in
  a kendra or trikona outside an enemy's sign) until a reviewer calibrates a Shadbala
  threshold.

When a rule is true, its evidence lists the parts that decided it, with the values
behind them, for example `house(lord(3)) in dusthanas (8; lord(3) = venus)`.

## Checks

- `engine/tests/test_rules_catalogue.py` runs every rule's positive, negative and
  cancelled charts, and checks the catalogue size and citations.
- `engine/tests/test_rules_properties.py` checks catalogue invariants on random charts
  and compares several rules with separate plain-Python implementations.
- `docs/ACCURACY.md` summarises the catalogue and its test results.

The bibliography, with the editions cited, is in [`sources.yaml`](sources.yaml).
